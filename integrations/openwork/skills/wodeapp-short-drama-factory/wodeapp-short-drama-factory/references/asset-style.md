# Asset Style Rules

Use one style bible across character, scene, storyboard, and video prompts.

## Live-Action Lock

For live-action short drama assets, include:

```text
Live-action photorealistic human actor casting reference.
Natural skin texture, real pores, realistic hands, practical wardrobe, actual camera photography, cinematic lens.
Not anime, not manga, not illustration, not semi-real illustration, not digital painting, not game concept art, not 3D render, not doll-like skin, not cosplay poster, not fantasy card art.
No celebrity likeness, no text, no logo, no watermark.
```

For character asset sheets:

```text
Unified live-action costume-fitting contact sheet.
Neutral charcoal-gray wet concrete studio.
Four full-body views on one sheet: front human, side human, back human, transformed/power form.
Same actor, same face, same hair, same body, same wardrobe, same marks.
Actual camera photography, fabric wrinkles, lens noise, slight human asymmetry.
Not a digital model sheet, not a game character turnaround, not a 3D render.
```

## Character Details

Every character prompt should state:

- Age and adult status.
- Ethnicity/casting anchor.
- Skin tone.
- Body type.
- Face structure.
- Hair and eyes.
- Wardrobe silhouette.
- Animal/power marker.
- Transformed or power form.
- Negative constraints.

## Transformation Rules

- Animal shifter transformed forms should be realistic animals with natural anatomy.
- Do not create humanoid animal monsters unless specifically requested.
- Humans in normal form should not grow wings, antlers, horns, or fantasy ears.
- Use jewelry, scars, tattoos, eye catchlights, fabric, or practical props for normal-form animal identity.

## Review Rule

Always inspect contact sheets before finalizing. If existing references mix live-action, 3D, and manga styles, regenerate the references; written style rules alone will not fix downstream video consistency.
